# QBCC — What Project Managers Need to Know

*Reference notes on the Queensland Building and Construction Commission (QBCC) as it applies to project managers on Queensland construction projects. Compiled July 2026. This is general information, not legal advice — check qbcc.qld.gov.au or a construction lawyer for anything you're relying on for a specific decision.*

## What the QBCC is

The QBCC is Queensland's building industry regulator. It licenses contractors, administers the state's home warranty insurance scheme, enforces minimum financial requirements, runs the trust account framework for progress payments, handles building disputes and defect complaints, and takes disciplinary action (fines, demerit points, licence suspension) against non-compliant licensees.

## Does a "project manager" need a QBCC licence?

It depends on what you actually do, not your job title. QBCC licensing is role-based:

- If you're **on site directing trades and supervising the work itself**, you need a **Site Supervisor** licence. This licence lets you oversee building work but not enter into contracts on the company's behalf.
- If you're **contracting directly with clients, administering contracts, or running the commercial/administrative side of a project** — including acting as a *superintendent* (certifying timing, quality, and cost under a contract) — you need a **Builder—Project Management Services** licence.
- If you're a **PAYG employee working under someone else's licence** (e.g. a project manager employed by a licensed builder, not contracting independently), you generally don't need your own licence for that work — the company's nominee/licensee carries the regulatory responsibility. This is the most common situation where "project manager" as a job title doesn't actually require a personal QBCC licence.

Get this wrong and you risk operating unlicensed, which is a real enforcement risk — see [Why getting the right QBCC licence matters](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/news/why-getting-right-qbcc-licence-matters).

## The relevant licence: Builder—Project Management Services

This is the licence class that actually covers "project manager" work in the QBCC system.

**Scope — includes:**
- Administration services
- Advisory services
- Management services (other than coordinating trade scheduling), including acting as agent for another person
- If appointed as **superintendent** under a contract: administering the contract on behalf of the principal, and certifying timing, quality, and cost matters

**Scope — does not include:** performing building work services under a construction management contract (that's a different licence class).

**Eligibility (Contractor, Nominee Supervisor, or Site Supervisor variants):**
- **Fit and proper person** test, permission to work in Queensland, photo ID, evidence of business structure
- **Technical qualifications** — apprenticeship papers, an RTO qualification, recognition of prior learning, or a qualification statement from an approved authority
- **4 years' experience** in the scope of work (or equivalent), verified by a suitably qualified referee
- **Minimum Financial Requirements (MFR)** — an MFR report or declaration, unless you qualify for the PI insurance exemption (see below)
- **Professional Indemnity (PI) insurance** — required for a contractor licence in this class

**PI insurance exemption from detailed financial reporting:** if you hold (or are only applying for) licences in Builder—Project Management Services, Building Design (low/medium rise/open), Hydraulic Services Design, or Site Classifier — and nothing else — you can submit an estimated maximum revenue declaration instead of a full MFR report. This does *not* apply if you also hold any other class of contractor licence, or if you operate under a trust.

**Application fees** (current as of this research; QBCC fees increase every 1 July):
- Individual contractor: ~$920–$1,697 depending on revenue category (~half price if you're already a QBCC licensee)
- Company: ~$1,673–$3,076 depending on revenue category
- Nominee/site supervisor: ~$501

**How to apply:** confirm the licence class covers your actual scope of work → gather technical qualification evidence, experience declaration, and referee sign-off → complete the relevant application form (individual/nominee or company) → lodge in person, by mail, or by email with the fee.

Source: [Builder—project management services](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/licences/apply-licence/available-licences/builder/builder-project-management-services)

## Financial reporting obligations (MFR)

If you hold this licence as a company (any revenue category) or as an individual with approved maximum revenue over $800,000, you must lodge annual MFR reporting. As of March 2025, sole traders/individuals in the lowest categories (SC1, up to $200k; SC2, up to $800k) no longer need to lodge annual reports — a significant simplification. Simplified financial statements (Special Purpose Financial Statements) are also now accepted for categories SC1, SC2, 1, 2 and 3. A significant change in your financial circumstances can still trigger a fresh MFR report requirement outside the annual cycle.

Source: [MFR and annual reporting](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/licences/maintain-your-licence/mfr-annual-reporting)

## Trust accounts & getting paid (Building Industry Fairness Act)

If you're administering contracts or certifying payments as a project manager/superintendent, this is the part that affects your day-to-day the most:

- Head contractors on eligible projects must operate a **Project Trust Account (PTA)** to hold progress payments and retention money for subcontractors, under the *Building Industry Fairness (Security of Payment) Act 2017*.
- To get paid, a contractor submits a **payment claim**; the respondent must either pay in full or issue a **payment schedule** within the statutory timeframe. QBCC administers **adjudication** if a payment dispute isn't resolved.
- PTA transactions (payment claims, receipts, invoices, payment schedules, bank statements) must be kept in detailed, timely records, and the trust account reconciled **at least monthly**.
- **Recent change:** the rollout of trust accounts to private projects under $10 million has been **paused** (part of the "Building Reg Reno" reforms, effective March 2025) — worth confirming current thresholds before assuming a project needs a PTA.

Source: [Trustee guide: project trusts](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/guide-trustee-project-trusts.pdf), [What's changed in the BIFOLA Act?](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/news/whats-changed-bifola-act)

## Compliance, defects, and discipline

- **Direction to Rectify (DTR):** after a site inspection, QBCC can direct a licensee to fix defective or incomplete work, typically within 35 days.
- **Demerit points:** offences (including failing to comply with a DTR) attract 2–10 points. **30 points within 3 years = 3-year disqualification from holding a licence.** A second disqualification within 10 years of the first = lifetime disqualification.
- Non-compliance can also lead to fines, tribunal/court prosecution, licence conditions, or suspension/cancellation via a show-cause process.
- **Serious safety incidents** must be reported — under the Tranche 3 reforms (effective 1 February 2026), reporting is now coordinated through a single point of truth via the Office of Industrial Relations, with data shared to QBCC. Penalties for failing to report increased from 80 to 100 penalty units.

Source: [Demerit points](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/non-compliance/consequences-non-compliance/demerit-points), [Direction to rectify](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/non-compliance/consequences-non-compliance/direction-rectify)

## Recent and upcoming regulatory changes ("Building Reg Reno")

Queensland has been rolling out a multi-tranche reform package:

- **Tranches 1 & 2 (March 2025):** removed annual MFR reporting for SC1/SC2 individuals; paused trust account rollout to private projects under $10M; pushed the passive fire licensing compliance deadline from May 2025 to May 2030.
- **Tranche 3 (commenced 1 February 2026):** digitises QBCC processes (less paper-based licensing/admin), consolidates serious-incident reporting through the Office of Industrial Relations with data-sharing to QBCC, and raises penalties for failing to report incidents.
- **Tranche 4 (proposed, 2026):** expected to update licensing thresholds, modernise home warranty insurance settings, and improve dispute resolution — not yet legislated as of this research.

Source: [Deconstructing the Building Reg Reno](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/licences/licensing-reforms/deconstructing-building-reg-reno), [Key changes under the QBCC Bill (HIA)](https://hia.com.au/our-industry/newsroom/industry-policy/2025/12/key-changes-under-the-qbcc-bill)

## Quick reference: things a PM should stay on top of

- Which licence (if any) their role actually requires, and whether the company's nominee/licensee arrangement covers them
- MFR/annual reporting deadlines if they hold a contractor-type licence
- PTA reconciliation cadence (monthly minimum) on any project running a trust account
- Payment claim / payment schedule statutory timeframes on every contract
- Any open Directions to Rectify and their 35-day clock
- Serious incident reporting obligations, especially given the February 2026 reporting-pathway change
- Watching for Tranche 4 changes later in 2026

## Addendum — July 2026 re-review

Re-checked against current QBCC guidance; the picture above still holds, with a few more specific details worth pinning down:

- **MFR annual reporting lodgement windows** (not just "annually" — specific dates): for financial categories **SC1/SC2**, the lodgement period opens **1 November** and reporting is due by **31 March**. For **categories 1–7**, the lodgement period opens **1 August** and is due by **31 December**. Miss it and QBCC can impose licence conditions, restrict turnover, or suspend/cancel the licence.
- **Current Ratio requirement:** all MFR-reportable licensees must maintain a current-assets-to-current-liabilities ratio of at least **1:1** — this is the core financial health test QBCC checks at annual reporting and can request evidence of at any time.
- **The SC1/SC2 reporting exemption is for individual sole traders only** — a company holding an SC1/SC2 licence still has to lodge annual reporting regardless of category. Worth double-checking which entity type actually holds the licence before assuming the exemption applies.
- **Project Trust Account payments are restricted to subcontractor beneficiaries only** — suppliers, employees, or contractors from other projects can't be paid out of a project's PTA, even if the head contractor is short on general operating funds elsewhere.
- **Fee structure is now two-part:** an application fee plus a separate ongoing licence fee (unless a waiver applies to your pathway) — budget for both, not just the headline application cost.
- **Tranche 3 (from 1 February 2026) is live:** QBCC now issues notices and correspondence by email as the default channel — licensees must keep their email address current with QBCC (update within 14 days of any change) or risk missing a formal notice, including Direction to Rectify correspondence.

Source: [MARCH 2026 regulatory guide — MFR annual reporting](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/regulatory-guide-mfr-annual-reporting.pdf), [Trustee guide: project trusts](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/guide-trustee-project-trusts.pdf), [Deconstructing the Building Reg Reno](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/licences/licensing-reforms/deconstructing-building-reg-reno)
